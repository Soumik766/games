What it is

ReelKitty is for anyone who has opened Instagram for a minute and looked up an hour later. It counts the reels and shorts you swipe through in Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook and Snapchat, shows the number in a small counter on your screen, and nudges you to stop: a daily limit, reminders after a long run, a reel-free Zen timer and a one-minute breathing break. It never blocks anything. It just makes the number hard to ignore.

  • A cat that keeps count It gets tired, then dizzy, then doomed as the day's reels add up, and says so in a speech bubble.
  • Help to stop A daily limit, stop reminders, Zen mode and a breathing break, all one tap from the counter.
  • Your week at a glance Reels per app, reel time, a 7-day chart and a home-screen widget showing the stats you pick.
  • Private by design No account. It cannot see your screen, and what it counts never leaves your phone.

Why it asks for Accessibility

To count reels, ReelKitty uses Android's Accessibility service. From it, the app receives only how far the screen scrolled and the name of the app being scrolled, and recognises a reel from the size of each swipe. It cannot read text, images or anything else on your screen, and it never taps or types for you. The same service draws the floating counter. The app explains this on its own screen and asks before turning anything on, and you can switch it off at any time in Android's settings.

Ads and purchases

ReelKitty is free and has no purchases. It shows ads inside its own screens: a banner, an occasional full-screen ad at a natural pause, and — only if you choose to watch one — a short video that unlocks the premium counter styles for a day. The floating counter never shows an ad, and nothing the app counts is ever given to advertisers.

What is collected, and by whom, is set out in the privacy policy. The rules you agree to when you use the app are in the terms of use.
